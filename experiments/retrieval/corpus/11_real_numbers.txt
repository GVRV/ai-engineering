Real numbers are all the numbers that can be shown on a number line. They include both rational and irrational numbers. Rational numbers can be written as a fraction, such as 1/2, 3, or -4. They include whole numbers, integers, fractions, and terminating or repeating decimals. Irrational numbers cannot be written as simple fractions and have decimal values that go on forever without repeating a pattern. Examples include √2 and π. Real numbers can be positive, negative, or zero. We use real numbers in many everyday situations, such as measuring distance, temperature, time, money, and weight. Understanding real numbers helps us compare quantities, solve mathematical problems, and describe measurements accurately.
